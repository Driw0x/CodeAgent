# M5 — RAG Quality & Grounding


[Project README](../README.md)


<a id="objectif"></a>
## Objective

Improve the quality of retrieval and the consistency between the retrieved sources and the responses generated by CodeAgent.

The primary scope of CodeAgent is already considered complete at M4. M5 constitutes a phase of improvement and evaluation of the RAG system.

<a id="benchmark"></a>
## Benchmark

The benchmark contains 25 questions covering the different components of the project.

Each question defines the expected chunks to be retrieved from:

- file
- chunk type
- name of the function, class, or variable

<a id="analyse-des-erreurs--baseline-m4"></a>
## Error analysis — Baseline M4

On 25 questions :

- 18 : relevant source present in the final top 5
- 5 : source absent from the 25 FAISS candidates
- 2: source present in the candidates FAISS but lost after reranking

The main improvement axis identified is therefore candidate retrieval.

<a id="hybrid-retrieval--baseline-m5"></a>
## Hybrid retrieval — Baseline M5

A hybrid dense + lexical search has been added to improve candidate coverage before reranking.

<a id="dataset-1"></a>
### Dataset

- Questions: 25
- Chunks: 111
- Embedding model: `sentence-transformers/all-MiniLM-L6-v2`
- Corpus: fixed fixture of CodeAgent

The results show an improvement in candidate retrieval compared to dense retrieval alone:

|Metric|Dense|Hybrid|
|---|---:|---:|
|Hit@1| 0.0800 | 0.1600 |
|Hit@3| 0.4400 | 0.4400 |
|Hit@5| 0.6400 | 0.6400 |
|Hit@10| 0.7200 | 0.8000 |
|Hit@25| 0.8000 | 0.8800 |
|Recall@25| 0.8000 | 0.8800 |
|MRR@25| 0.3042 | 0.3574 |

Hybrid planning primarily improves coverage of medium and long trajectory candidates for `k`. The number of queries for which no relevant source is present among the 25 candidates remains limited to 3.

After fusion with lexical reranking via RRF 1:1, the pipeline achieves:

|Metric|M5 hybrid + reranking|
|---|---:|
|Hit@1| 0.3600 |
|Hit@3| 0.7200 |
|Hit-5| 0.8400 |
|Recall@5| 0.8000 |
|MRR@5| 0.5413 |

Error analysis shows:

- 21 questions correctly solved in the top 5 final
- 3 candidate retrieval failures
- 1 reranking failure

The hybrid search thus improves candidate coverage, while the RRF reranking effectively repositions relevant chunks within the top 5 final.

<a id="grounding"></a>
## Grounding

M5 adds citations `[Sx]`, automatic validation, and claim-by-claim verification of assertion support by cited sources.

The same local LLM is used for generation and validation. This choice maintains a simple local architecture, but may produce correlated errors.

<a id="évaluation-finale-m4-vs-m5"></a>
## Final evaluation M4 vs M5

The final holdout contains 16 questions, 12 of which have expected sources.

|Metric|M4|M5|
|---|---:|---:|
|Hit@1| 0.4167 | 0.1667 |
|Hit@3| 0.5833 | 0.5833 |
|Hit@5| 0.6667 | 0.8333 |
|Recall@5| 0.6667 | 0.8333 |
|MRR@5| 0.5069 | 0.3917 |
|Response behavior match| 0.6250 | 0.7500 |

M5 improves top-5 coverage and response/abstention behavior, but degrades early ranking. The end-to-end average latency increases from `4.36 s` for M4 to `6.90 s` for M5, a difference of `+2.54 s`.

On M5, 8 responses were verified by grounding: 6 were supported and 2 rejected. No invalid citations were observed.

The final holdout is fixed and should no longer be used to retrain M5.

M6 then reuses this grounding to validate tool-based response answers. On a dedicated balanced holdout of 40 cases, `Qwen2.5-Coder 14B` achieves `97.5 %` accuracy, `100 %` recall on unsupported cases, 0 false accepts and 1 false reject; it is retained as grounder M6. The final end-to-end benchmark M6 achieves `80 %` success with 0 `citation_error`, the remaining failures being rejections `unsupported`.

<a id="future-improvements"></a>
## Future improvements

- a specialized NLI or verifier model only if a benchmark shows measurable improvement over the current grounder;
- improvement of top-1 ranking;
- reduction of latency of local LLMs and cold starts when multiple models cannot remain resident.
